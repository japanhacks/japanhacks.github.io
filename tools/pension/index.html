5-Step Official Tax & Pension Refund Timeline

1Before Leaving Japan
Surrender your resident registration (転出届) at your local city hall and appoint a Tax Representative (納税管理人 Nōzei Kanri-nin) who resides in Japan. Submit the "Declaration of Tax Representative" (納税管理人の届出書) to your jurisdiction tax office.
2Submit Lump-Sum Claim to Japan Pension Service
Mail your Lump-Sum Withdrawal Claim Form (脱退一時金請求書) along with your original Pension Booklet (年金手帳) or Basic Pension Number Notification, copy of passport (exit stamp page), and bank confirmation letter to Japan Pension Service in Chiba.
3Receive Initial Bank Transfer & Blue/Pink Slip
Approx. 3 to 5 months later, Japan Pension Service wires the 80% net refund to your overseas bank account and mails the original "Notice of Payment of Lump-Sum Withdrawal" (脱退一時金支給決定通知書) slip to your home address abroad.
4Mail Original Notice of Payment Slip to Tax Representative
Mail the original physical "Notice of Payment" slip (DO NOT send a copy) to your Tax Representative in Japan. The tax office requires the original document.
5Tax Rep Files Kakutei Shinkoku for 20.42% Refund
Your Tax Representative files a Final Income Tax Return (確定申告 Kakutei Shinkoku) at the tax office. The 20.42% withheld tax (¥235,851) is deposited into your Tax Rep's Japanese bank account to transfer to you.

Expert Legal & Accounting Advisories

  • Must apply within 2 years of leaving Japan and forfeiting your Japanese address registration (住民票の除票).
  • 20.42% non-resident income tax is withheld at source by Japan Pension Service.
  • Appoint a Tax Representative (納税管理人 Nōzei Kanri-nin) in Japan to file a Kakutei Shinkoku (確定申告) and recover the full 20.42% tax (¥235,851).
  • Note: Claiming the lump-sum withdrawal resets your total Japanese pension contribution months to 0, which waives future Japanese pension eligibility.
